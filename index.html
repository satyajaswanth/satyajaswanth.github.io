About

Computing at the edge of power

I am an Assistant Professor in Computer Science, working on computer architecture, embedded AI, and hardware–software co-design for ultra-constrained edge devices. My research asks a hard question: how do you run useful computation — including deep-learning inference — on devices that have no battery and lose power without warning?

I completed my PhD at the Indian Institute of Technology Ropar (2018–2023) under the guidance of Dr. Neeraj Goel and Dr. Mukesh Saini, with a thesis on the design of non-volatile processors for intermittent computing. I then joined imec in Leuven, Belgium as a Postdoctoral Researcher, continuing work on memory-aware inference for energy-harvesting systems.

My work spans the memory hierarchy — from STT-RAM hybrid caches to FRAM-based main memory — and the microcontrollers that sit at the heart of battery-less platforms. The through-line is intermittency-robust design: keeping state safe across power failures while staying within a tiny energy budget.

Research Interests

Where memory meets energy

Intermittent Computing

Keeping programs correct across frequent, unpredictable power failures on energy-harvesting devices — checkpointing, safe resumption, and forward progress under tight energy budgets.

power-failure resiliencecheckpointing

Non-Volatile Memory

Architecting with PCM-RAM, STT-RAM, and FRAM: placement and migration policies for hybrid caches, and NVM-based main memory that retains state through power loss.

STT-RAMFRAMPCM-RAM

Non-Volatile Processors

Designing and validating processors on real platforms (MSP430FR6989, MSP430F5529) that save and restore machine state efficiently for battery-less operation.

MSP430energy harvesting

Embedded AI & TinyML

Memory-aware scheduling of CNN inference and intermittency-robust deep learning for microcontroller-class hardware — bringing inference to devices that run on harvested power.

on-device inferenceHW/SW co-design
